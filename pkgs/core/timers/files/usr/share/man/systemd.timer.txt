systemd.timer - start a service on a schedule

A timer starts a service now and then, like cron. It lives next to the
service, as /etc/systemd/system/<name>.timer, and starts <name>.service
unless Unit= names another one:

  [Unit]
  Description=Back up every night

  [Timer]
  OnCalendar=daily          at midnight (see below)
  OnBootSec=2min            once, two minutes after boot
  OnUnitActiveSec=1h        again an hour after each start
  Unit=backup.service       the service to start (default: same name)

A timer needs at least one of OnCalendar, OnBootSec or OnUnitActiveSec.
The service it starts usually does its job and ends (Restart=no).

OnCalendar
  hourly  daily  weekly     every hour, at midnight, Monday at midnight
  14:30                     every day at half past two
  *:15                      every hour at quarter past
  *:00/10                   every ten minutes
  Mon,Fri 08:00             Mondays and Fridays at eight
The times are local (see timedatectl); without an internet card they are
the Minecraft world's time.

Spans
  90  30s  5min  2h  1d  1h 30min

COMMANDS
  timers                         every timer, when it runs next and last ran
  sudo timers enable <name>      turn it on
  sudo timers disable <name>
  sudo timers start <name>       start its service now

timerd, a service, does the starting; installing the timers package
enabled it. Each start shows up in journalctl -u timerd.

SEE ALSO
  man systemd.service, man timers
